Strava Shoe Finder

Diagnosing Low Adoption of a New Feature

UX Researcher
Timeline: Mar–Apr 2025

Initial Research Focus

The Problem

A new shoe recommender, powered by Strava, was launched to 10% of US users, but initial quantitative data showed low adoption. The team needed to urgently understand why users were not engaging with the feature and what usability or trust barriers were preventing them from using it.

The Goal

How might we diagnose the reasons for low adoption and identify the critical issues with the Strava shoe finder to improve the experience, build user trust, and ultimately drive engagement?

What We Learned

"Should I trust on the results? That's the question."

- Participant Quote

Technical Issues Severely Eroded Trust

3 out of 4 participants who connected felt their data was inaccurate (reporting lower activity levels). Combined with connectivity problems for 2 participants, this created immediate skepticism.

The Core Concept is Strongly Validated

Despite the issues, all 5 participants were positive about the idea and said they would use it. They found the "modern" and "cool" aesthetic appealing and valued recommendations based on their own data.

Poor Pacing & Interaction

participants felt the story screens moved too quickly to absorb the information. They also found that the tap-to-pause interaction was not intuitive.

The initial entry point was not clear

causing participants to mistake it for an advertisement banner.

Exploring deeper opportunities for improvement

Control & Personalization

Users wanted to fine-tune the data, such as selecting a timeframe or run type, to match their current goals, not just their history.

Transparency & Justification

"Why this shoe for me?" Users needed to understand the logic behind the "% match" to trust the recommendation.

Context & Comparison

Seeing a top 3 was good, but users craved the ability to easily compare the recommended shoes side-by-side to make a final decision.

Project Outcome & Impact

75%

of Users Felt Their
Data Was Inaccurate

Delivered a data-driven report that explained the low adoption rates seen in analytics. The qualitative insights pinpointed the specific trust and usability issues causing the drop-off, providing a clear roadmap to prioritize critical fixes.

Though the project was ultimately deprioritized, I advocated for the core findings, especially around data transparency and user control, to be adopted as principles for other data driven personalisation features across the company. (As the shoe recommender is a recuring topic)

Health Metric

Because it was not relaunched, even though the research strongly validated the concept, we missed the opportunity to compare click rates and conversion rates after addressing all the highlighted issues from the research.

Why This Project Matters

This project highlights the power of using qualitative research to explain the 'why' behind quantitative data. While analytics showed what was happening (low adoption), our interviews revealed the critical trust and usability issues that were causing it, transforming a confusing metric into something actionable.

What I’d Improve

A key takeaway for me is the potential value of this research when conducted earlier. In the future, when possible, I would advocate for a pre-launch study, even if it is a fast unmoderated one.
